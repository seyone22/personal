> **Pilot study (Data collected: November 18, 2026).** Daily supermarket prices were pulled from `food.seyone.dev` (`foodrepo`) across Cargills, Keells, Arpico, and SPAR, and benchmarked against Department of Census and Statistics (DCS) and Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) CCPI (2021=100) releases through October 2026.

Across **43 CCPI-matched supermarket SKUs** covering **54.9% of Sri Lanka's official food basket**, our weighted pilot index rose **`+9.25%`** between June 1 and October 31, 2026 — and jumped another **`+4.19%` in the first 18 days of November** (`Mar 24 → Nov 18: +21.22%` vs. `Mar 24 → Oct 31: +16.34%`).

At first glance, `+9.25%` looks right next to CBSL's **October 2026 headline food inflation of `+8.8%`** (overall CCPI headline: `8.3%`, up from `2.2%` in March). **That resemblance is a statistical trap.** CBSL's `+8.8%` is a **12-month year-on-year** figure (Oct 2025 → Oct 2026), whereas our `+9.25%` is a **4-month cumulative** supermarket move (Jun → Oct 2026). Comparing apples to apples against the official DCS Food &amp; Non-Alcoholic Beverages index over the exact same June–October 2026 window tells a sharper story.

## Apples-to-Apples: 4-Month Supermarket Surge vs. Official DCS Food Index

In DCS's CCPI (2021=100) series, the **Food &amp; Non-Alcoholic Beverages** sub-index barely moved between June and October 2026 — rising just **`+1.12%` cumulative** (`258.2` to `261.1`). The jump in official YoY food inflation from `3.6%` in June to `8.8%` in October was driven largely by 2025 base effects (most visibly in August) rather than a steep mid-year surge in the official food index itself:

| Month (2026) | DCS Food Index (`2021=100`) | DCS Food MoM | DCS Food Cum. (vs. Jun) | DCS Food YoY | CBSL Headline YoY |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **June** | `258.2` | — | `0.00%` | `3.6%` | `6.8%` |
| **July** | `258.2` | `0.00%` | `0.00%` | `6.3%` | `7.3%` |

*\*Calculated from published 1-decimal DCS index points (`261.0 → 261.1`); DCS reported a `+0.02%` unrounded monthly food contribution in October.*

Over that identical four-month window (June to October 2026), our 43-SKU supermarket pilot rose **`+9.25%`** — **more than 8x faster** than the official DCS Food index's `+1.12%`. That divergence is not a bug; it reflects two structural differences:

1. **Channel skew:** Online modern-trade supermarkets (Cargills, Keells, Arpico, SPAR) reprice volatile produce faster than the broader mix of open-air *pola* markets and traditional grocers surveyed by DCS.
2. **Basket coverage:** Our matched slice captures `54.9%` of the food basket (heavily represented by packaged staples and fresh produce) while excluding `45.1%` — including fresh sea fish and fresh chicken, where price movements dampened the official index.

## Weighting by the Official CCPI (2021=100) Basket

In the DCS CCPI (2021=100) basket (derived from the 2019 Household Income and Expenditure Survey), **Food &amp; Non-Alcoholic Beverages** carries a `26.23%` share of total CPI (`Non-Food` is `73.77%`), led by rice (`2.72%` of total CPI, ~`10.4%` of food), milk powder (`1.71%`, ~`6.5%`), coconut (`1.38%`, ~`5.3%`), mysore dhal (`0.50%`), and sugar (`0.45%`), with individual fresh vegetables and spices typically weighted between `0.03%` and `0.35%` each (e.g., big onions `0.35%`, beans `0.25%`, chilli powder `0.25%`, lime `0.08%`).

To aggregate our **43 matched SKUs** (`14.41%` of total CPI, or **54.9%** of the `26.23%` food basket) without letting missing categories distort the scale, the pilot computes a **Laspeyres-style re-normalized weighted price relative** across the matched subset:

```text
Pilot Food Index (t vs. t0) = [ Σ (w_i × (P_i,t / P_i,0)) / Σ w_i ] - 1

where:
  w_i   = official DCS CCPI (2021=100) expenditure weight for SKU i
  Σ w_i = 14.41% of total CPI (54.9% of the 26.23% Food group)
  P_i,t = observed supermarket retail price on date t (normalized to per-kg/unit)
```

For transparency, the table below summarizes the **six daily series exported in this post's interactive dataset** (`sl-grocery-history.json`), showing both their full tracked spans and their October 31 → November 18 moves:

Rolling all **43 matched SKUs** through that re-normalized formula produces three period readings:

- **Jun 01 → Oct 31, 2026:** **`+9.25%`** (vs. DCS Food index `+1.12%` cumulative over Jun–Oct, and CBSL's `+8.8%` 12-month YoY food inflation).
- **Mar 24 → Oct 31, 2026:** **`+16.34%`**.
- **Mar 24 → Nov 18, 2026:** **`+21.22%`** — implying a **`+4.19%` single-month-to-date jump in 18 days** (`1.2122 / 1.1634 - 1`), driven by an early-November surge in fresh vegetables, Red Onions (**+200.0%**, `Rs. 340` to `Rs. 1,020/kg`), and Big Onions (`+104.5%`).

## Item-for-Item Comparison Against CBSL Releases (and a November Leading Indicator)

Comparing our 43-item slice directly against the specific food drivers CBSL and DCS cited in September and October 2026 shows where modern-trade data lines up with official releases — and where it moves ahead of them:

- **Matching upward drivers:** DCS flagged **limes**, **coconuts**, **big onions**, **red onions**, **milk powder**, **chilli powder**, and **normal bread** as primary September–October upward drivers. In `food.seyone.dev` through Oct 31, Cargills Lime was **+71.9%**, SPAR Coconut **+21.0%**, Big Onions **+95.5%** (`Mar→Oct`) / **+43.3%** (`Jun→Oct`), Red Onions **+94.1%** (`Mar→Oct`) / **+40.4%** (`Jun→Oct`), Anchor Milk Powder **+13.0%** (`Mar→Oct`) / **+8.7%** (`Jun→Oct`), Chilli Powder **+19.0%**, and Finagle Bread **+8.1%**.
- **Offsetting drops:** Keells Red Dhal (`-12.0%`), Red Kekulu Rice (`-8.3%`), Baby Carrots (`-34.1%`), and Baby Potatoes (`-9.1%`) declined in supermarket pricing, balancing out the sharper produce spikes.
- **Dated leading indicator for November 2026 CCPI (logged Nov 18):** While our six-item chart shows chili and dhal cooling in early November, the broader 43-SKU weighted index jumped **`+4.19%` between October 31 and November 18** (`Mar→Oct +16.34%` to `Mar→Nov +21.22%`). That move is led by fresh vegetables — Beetroot (`+500.0%`), Cabbage (`+347.4%`), Bitter Gourd (`+281.8%`), Cucumber (`+250.0%`) — and Red Onions (`Rs. 1,020/kg` by Nov 18). Because DCS publishes the November 2026 CCPI at month-end, this creates a falsifiable test: **if supermarket produce leads Colombo retail markets as expected, DCS's November Food index should break noticeably above October's `261.1` (after October's near-flat `+0.04%` MoM print) and cite fresh vegetables and red onions among its primary upward contributors.**

## Current Coverage Gaps (The Remaining 45.1%)

Because this is a pilot built strictly on online supermarket listings, **45.1%** of the CCPI food basket is still unmapped or excluded:

- **Fresh fish (`~2.8%+`):** Keells and the tracked online supermarkets do not list fresh `Thalapath` (`0.84%`) or `Kelawalla` (`0.74%`) consistently online, only dry and canned fish (`0.0%` change).
- **Fresh chicken (`1.25%`) and fresh fruits:** Online product search either returned processed items (sausages, snacks, dehydrated fruit) or lacked clean 200-day series.

Closing those fresh-market gaps (especially fresh fish and fresh chicken) is the next step for `food.seyone.dev`, alongside automating unit normalization (`250g` packs vs. `1kg` bulk) across chains.
